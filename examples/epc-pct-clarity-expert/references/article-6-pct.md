# Article 6 PCT — International Stage Clarity

## Text

> **Article 6 PCT — The Claims**
>
> The claim or claims shall **define the matter for which protection is sought**. Claims shall be **clear and concise**. They shall be **fully supported by the description**.

(Patent Cooperation Treaty, 1970, as amended)

## Relationship to Article 84 EPC

The wording is **almost identical** to Article 84 EPC. The substantive standard is the same: claims must define, be clear, be concise, and be supported. The differences are **procedural and consequential**, not substantive.

| Aspect | Article 84 EPC | Article 6 PCT |
|---|---|---|
| Adjudicating body | EPO Examining/Opposition Divisions; Boards of Appeal | International Searching Authority (ISA) and International Preliminary Examining Authority (IPEA) |
| Consequence of non-compliance | Refusal (Art. 97(2) EPC), revocation in opposition limited by G 3/14 | **Written opinion** under Rule 43bis (ISA) or Art. 33–34 (IPEA); **no refusal** — the application proceeds to national/regional phase regardless |
| Binding effect | Binding within EPO proceedings | **Non-binding** on designated/elected Offices; each national/regional Office re-examines under its own law |
| Reformulation power | Examiner can require amendments | ISA can only issue an opinion; amendments under Art. 19 PCT (claims) or Art. 34 PCT (full application during Chapter II) |

## Rule 6 PCT — Implementing Details

Rule 6 PCT specifies the formal claim requirements (analogous to Rule 43 EPC). Key points:

- **Rule 6.1** — number and numbering of claims; reasonable in number having regard to the nature of the invention
- **Rule 6.2** — references to descriptions / drawings: claims shall not, except where absolutely necessary, rely "in respect of the technical features of the invention" on references such as "as described in part … of the description" or "as illustrated in figure … of the drawings"
- **Rule 6.3** — **two-part form** ("characterised in that …") recommended where appropriate; not mandatory
- **Rule 6.4** — dependent claims: must contain a reference to the other claim(s) and state the additional features
- **Rule 6.5** — Markush-type claims permitted under the same standard as substantive PCT examination
  
Rule 6 PCT does **not** define what "clear", "concise" or "supported" mean — that comes from Article 6 PCT and the PCT International Search and Preliminary Examination Guidelines (PCT-ISPE Guidelines).

## PCT-ISPE Guidelines (clarity at the international stage)

The **PCT International Search and Preliminary Examination Guidelines** (the "PCT-ISPE Guidelines", current edition 2024) — Chapter 5 covers claim clarity. The substantive criteria mirror EPO Guidelines F-IV closely, because the EPO is the most active ISA/IPEA and its practice influences PCT practice broadly.

Notable points:
- The ISA examines clarity **to the extent that lack of clarity impedes the search** (PCT Rule 39 / PCT-ISPE Guidelines 5.42–5.46): if the claim is so unclear that no meaningful search is possible, the ISA may issue a partial search or a declaration of non-establishment under Art. 17(2) PCT.
- The IPEA in Chapter II examines clarity substantively but again only issues an opinion, not a refusal.

## The Two Practical Consequences for the Practitioner

### 1. ISA clarity opinion does not bind national phase

A negative written opinion on clarity from the ISA does **not refuse** the application. The applicant can:
- Amend under Art. 19 PCT (claims only, once, after international search report)
- Amend under Art. 34 PCT (during Chapter II)
- Or **wait** until national/regional phase entry and amend then

Each national/regional Office re-examines clarity under **its own law** (Art. 84 EPC at EPO, §112 USC at USPTO, etc.). A claim found clear by the ISA may be objected to at the EPO and vice versa.

### 2. Search-blocking unclear claims attract Art. 17(2)(a) PCT consequences

Where the ISA cannot perform a meaningful search because the claims are so vague (typical: pure result-to-be-achieved claims, "data-defined" inventions, etc.), the ISA may **decline to search** part or all of the claims and issue a partial search or non-establishment declaration. This significantly weakens the application going into national phase.

## Practical Strategy for PCT Filings Heading to EPO

If your PCT application is destined for EP regional phase, **draft to Article 84 EPC standards from the start** — the EPO is the strictest ISA on clarity, and the EP examination will apply Art. 84 directly. Drafting to PCT-only clarity standards risks pushing fixes into expensive EP prosecution.

**Filing-stage rules of thumb**:
- Use EPC-clean language (no "substantially", "approximately" without defined tolerance)
- Avoid mere result-to-be-achieved features
- Make every parameter measurable with a stated method
- Pair functional features with structural backbone
- Define unusual terms in the description **and** import the definition into the claim where it matters

## Comparison Table — clarity standards across major Offices

| Office | Statutory base | Standard | Examines clarity at substantive stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EPO** | Art. 84 EPC | Strict; claim alone test | Yes — primary ground in examination |
| **USPTO** | 35 USC §112(b) | "Particular and distinct" — broadly comparable | Yes — frequent rejection ground |
| **JPO** | Patent Act Art. 36(6)(ii) | Comparable; specific enumeration of issues | Yes |
| **CNIPA** | Patent Law Art. 26.4 | Comparable | Yes |
| **PCT/ISA** | Art. 6 PCT, Rule 6 PCT | Same substantive standard as EPC | Opinion only — non-binding |

**Bottom line**: clarity is a worldwide patentability requirement. A claim that passes EPO Art. 84 generally passes everywhere else; the converse is not always true.
