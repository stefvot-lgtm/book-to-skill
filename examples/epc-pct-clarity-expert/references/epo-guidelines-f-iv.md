# EPO Guidelines for Examination — Part F, Chapter IV (Claims)

This file maps the **EPO Guidelines for Examination** (Part F — *The European Patent Application*, Chapter IV — *Claims*) section by section, with focus on clarity. Citations follow the **March 2024 edition** (the most recent at the time of writing; the Guidelines are revised annually and published in the Official Journal of the EPO). Always verify the current edition: https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/guidelines.html

> Guidelines status: they are **internal instructions** to EPO examiners. They are **persuasive but not binding** on the Boards of Appeal (Art. 23(3) EPC implementing regulations; numerous Board decisions). Where Board case law diverges, the case law prevails.

---

## F-IV, 1 — General Remarks

**Scope**: introduces the role of claims under Art. 84 EPC — they define the matter for which protection is sought.

**Categories of claim** (F-IV-1, F-IV-3.1):
- **Product / entity** — a thing (apparatus, device, composition, compound, system)
- **Activity / method / process / use** — a step or sequence

A single application may contain multiple categories. Multiple **independent** claims of the same category are restricted by **Rule 43(2) EPC** with three exceptions: (a) interrelated products, (b) different uses of a product/apparatus, (c) alternatives where covering them by a single claim is inappropriate.

---

## F-IV, 2 — Form and Content of Claims

Rules 43–47 EPC. Key points:
- **Rule 43(1)** — two-part form recommended where appropriate (preamble: prior art; characterising portion: "characterised in that …")
- **Rule 43(3)** — independent + dependent claim structure
- **Rule 43(4)** — dependent claims refer to one or more preceding claims and state additional features
- **Rule 43(5)** — number of claims reasonable
- **Rule 43(6)** — no reliance on description/drawings to define technical features, except where absolutely necessary
- **Rule 43(7)** — reference signs in parentheses; do **not** limit the claim

→ Cross-ref: [Rule 43 EPC](rule-43-epc.md)

## F-IV, 3 — Kinds of Claim

- **F-IV-3.1** — categories (product, process, use, apparatus, method)
- **F-IV-3.2** — independent and dependent
- **F-IV-3.3** — claims of different categories — combinations
- **F-IV-3.4** — independent claims of same category (Rule 43(2))
- **F-IV-3.5** — multiple dependencies — permitted; numbering carefully
- **F-IV-3.6** — alternative features
- **F-IV-3.7** — "preamble" and "characterising portion" two-part form
- **F-IV-3.8** — use claims (Art. 54(5) EPC for second medical use)
- **F-IV-3.9** — claims directed to computer-implemented inventions (cross-ref **G 3/08**, **T 1173/97**)

## F-IV, 4 — Clarity and Interpretation of Claims (THE central clarity chapter)

This is the chapter every practitioner must know. Detailed sub-sections:

### F-IV, 4.1 — General Remarks
The claim must be clear **in itself** when read by the skilled person with the common general knowledge **at the filing date**. Clarity is examined claim-by-claim and as a set.

### F-IV, 4.2 — Interpretation of Claims
For Art. 84 examination: the claim is interpreted as the skilled person would understand it. Description and drawings **may be used** to construe ambiguous terms with established special meaning, but **cannot rescue** a genuinely unclear claim.

> ⚠️ This is a frequent confusion. Art. 69 EPC (scope of protection for infringement) uses description + drawings. Art. 84 (clarity examination) requires the claim to stand on its own. The standard textbook reference: **T 1018/02**, **T 56/04**.

### F-IV, 4.3 — Inconsistencies Between Claims and Description
Two types:
1. **Different terminology** for the same feature
2. **Embodiments described** that fall **outside** the claim scope

The Guidelines (2024 edition) require the description to be amended to (a) use consistent terminology and (b) mark out-of-scope embodiments as "not according to the invention" or delete them.

> **CONTROVERSY** — Board case law on description adaptation is split:
> - **T 1989/18**: description adaptation cannot be required if claims are clear
> - **T 1024/18, T 1473/19**: description adaptation IS required to ensure Art. 84 support
> - **T 56/21**: referral pending on this issue
> Practitioner strategy: comply with examiner request to avoid refusal, but preserve the issue for appeal if a key file is affected.

→ Cross-ref: [case-law-clarity.md § Description Adaptation](case-law-clarity.md#description-adaptation-controversy)

### F-IV, 4.4 — General Statements, "Spirit" of the Invention
**Disallowed**:
- "The invention provides …" within the claim
- "in the spirit of the invention"
- "according to the present teaching"

→ Cross-ref: [clarity-objections-catalog.md § 8](clarity-objections-catalog.md#8-spirit-of-the-invention--the-invention-wording)

### F-IV, 4.5 — Essential Features
The independent claim must contain **all features essential to the invention**. Omitting an essential feature → Art. 84 objection.

**Test**: would the skilled person, without inventive effort, identify additional features as necessary to solve the technical problem? If yes — those features are essential.

**Case law**: **T 0032/82** (essential features), **T 0133/85**, **T 0922/05**.

### F-IV, 4.6 — Relative Terms

- "thin", "thick", "strong", "weak", "small", "large", "high", "low"
- Acceptable if "the meaning is sufficiently well established in the field"
- Otherwise: replace with measurable parameter

### F-IV, 4.6.1 — "About", "Approximately", "Substantially"

- Acceptable only where the term does not impair clarity in context
- The description should define the tolerance ("substantially perpendicular" = within X° of 90°)
- **Drafting tip**: import the tolerance into the claim

### F-IV, 4.7 — Terms like "approximately" (merged with 4.6.1 in current edition)

### F-IV, 4.8 — Trademarks

- Discouraged
- Acceptable only if the trademarked product has a constant, well-defined composition AND the description spells out the relevant properties

→ Cross-ref: [clarity-objections-catalog.md § 5](clarity-objections-catalog.md#5-trademarks)

### F-IV, 4.9 — Optional Features

- "preferably", "such as", "for example", "in particular", "and optionally" within the claim body → **delete** or move to dependent claim
- These create ambiguity about what is required

→ Cross-ref: [clarity-objections-catalog.md § 1](clarity-objections-catalog.md#preferably-such-as-for-example-in-particular)

### F-IV, 4.10 — Result to Be Achieved

- A claim defined only by the result is generally objectionable
- Acceptable only if (a) it is the only way to define the invention reasonably, AND (b) the result is measurable and reproducible

**Case law**: **T 68/85**, **T 939/92**, **T 1173/97**.

### F-IV, 4.11 — Parameters

- Standard parameters: OK (define measurement method)
- **Unusual parameters** — also a potential Art. 83 issue:
  - Must be measurable with a clearly specified method
  - Method must be reproducible
  - Justification required for using a non-standard parameter
- The EPO views "exotic parameters" with suspicion — they often hide novelty/clarity problems

**Case law**: **T 0094/82**, **T 728/98**, **T 0276/06**, **T 1845/14**.

### F-IV, 4.12 — Product-by-Process Claims

- Admissible only if the product cannot be defined otherwise
- The product itself must be novel — the process is irrelevant to novelty
- "Obtainable by" vs "obtained by" — equivalent for EPO (both impose the product itself, not the process)

**Case law**: **T 0150/82**, **T 0205/83**, **T 0219/83**, **T 0411/89**.

### F-IV, 4.13 — Apparatus / Method "for" doing something — Intended Use

Important distinction:
- **Apparatus claim**: "Apparatus *for* X" — the "for X" is **not limiting** if the apparatus could perform X (intended use)
- **Method claim**: "Method *for* X" — the "for X" **is limiting** (defines purpose)

This affects novelty and clarity in different ways.

**Case law**: **T 287/86**, **T 401/95**.

### F-IV, 4.14 — Definition by Reference to Use or Another Entity

A claim may define a product by reference to another entity ("a port adapted to receive a USB-C connector"). The reference entity must be clearly identifiable.

### F-IV, 4.15 — The Expression "in"

"X in Y" can be ambiguous (is Y limiting?). Prefer "X comprising Y" or "X within Y".

### F-IV, 4.16 — References to Description or Drawings in Claims (Rule 43(6))

- Avoid "as shown in Figure 3", "as described in paragraph 12"
- Only "absolutely necessary" references allowed
- Drafting strategy: convert the referenced content into claim language

### F-IV, 4.17 — Reference Signs

- Required where drawings exist (Rule 43(7))
- Do **not** limit scope (final sentence of Rule 43(7))
- Practitioner trap: missing reference signs → formalities objection

### F-IV, 4.18 — Negative Limitations / Disclaimers

Two categories per **G 1/03**, **G 2/03**, refined by **G 1/16**:
1. **Disclosed disclaimers** — based on disclosed subject-matter — generally OK
2. **Undisclosed disclaimers** — added during prosecution to restore novelty over accidental anticipation or to remove Art. 53 / Art. 54(3) subject-matter — strict admissibility conditions

The disclaimer must be **clear** in its boundary.

→ Cross-ref: [clarity-objections-catalog.md § 9](clarity-objections-catalog.md#9-disclaimers), [case-law-clarity.md § Disclaimers](case-law-clarity.md#disclaimer-jurisprudence)

## F-IV, 5 — Conciseness, Number of Claims

- A reasonable number of claims, having regard to the nature of the invention
- No fixed upper limit — but claims fees apply beyond 15 (Rule 45 EPC)
- Repetitive or unnecessary claims attract objection

## F-IV, 6 — Support in the Description (the Art. 84 support arm)

Distinct from clarity but in the same article. The description must support the **full claim breadth**:
- **F-IV-6.1** — general principle: claim scope ≤ disclosure scope
- **F-IV-6.2** — broad claims with narrow disclosure: unsupported
- **F-IV-6.3** — Selection inventions: sub-range must be supported
- **F-IV-6.4** — Generic claims with one example: presumption of insufficient support unless extrapolation is justified
- **F-IV-6.5** — Functional claims: support requires multiple structural means or convincing rationale

Support often overlaps with **Art. 83 sufficiency**. The EPO Examining Division typically combines both grounds where the breadth issue is also a reproducibility issue.

**Case law**: **T 409/91**, **T 435/91**, **T 939/92**, **T 1329/04**, **T 1063/06**, **T 1727/12**.

→ Cross-ref: [related-provisions.md § Art. 83](related-provisions.md#article-83-epc--sufficiency)

## How EPO Examiners Use the Guidelines

In practice, an EPO Art. 84 objection will:
1. Cite the specific Guidelines section (e.g. "see F-IV-4.6.1 regarding 'substantially'")
2. Identify the unclear term / passage
3. Sometimes cite a Board decision if the term has been litigated
4. Indicate what would resolve the objection

**Response strategy** (see [response-strategies.md](response-strategies.md)):
1. Read the Guidelines section cited — often the resolution is suggested there
2. Check the most recent Guidelines edition (the examiner may be citing an outdated version)
3. Check whether case law has refined the Guidelines position
4. Either amend or argue, but address the cited section explicitly

---

## Guidelines vs Case Law — when they diverge

The Guidelines reflect EPO **first-instance** practice. The Boards of Appeal are not bound. Notable areas of past divergence:
- **Description adaptation** (Guidelines say required; T 1989/18 says not always)
- **Plausibility for inventive step** — Guidelines updated 2024 to align with **G 2/21**
- **Computer-implemented inventions** — Guidelines occasionally lag case law on technical effect

**Drafting recommendation**: comply with Guidelines during examination (it's the path of least friction); preserve case-law arguments for appeal.
