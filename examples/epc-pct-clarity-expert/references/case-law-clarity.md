# Key Boards of Appeal Case Law on Clarity

Selected decisions of the **Enlarged Board** and **Technical Boards of Appeal** of the EPO that shape Art. 84 practice. Citations to OJ EPO and *Case Law of the Boards of Appeal* (CLBA, 10th edition, 2022 — "the White Book") where available.

> Numbering convention: "G x/yy" = Enlarged Board; "T x/yy" = Technical Board; "J x/yy" = Legal Board. The number after the slash is the year of filing the appeal.

---

## Enlarged Board Decisions

### G 3/14 (24 March 2015) — Clarity in opposition
**Question**: Can claims of a granted patent be examined for compliance with Art. 84 EPC in opposition proceedings when the amendment is a combination of granted claims?

**Holding**: In opposition, the claims of the patent **as granted** can be examined for compliance with Art. 84 EPC **only when, and then only to the extent that, the amendment introduces non-compliance with Art. 84**.

**Practitioner consequence**:
- Pure combination of granted claims → clarity NOT examinable in opposition (immune to attack on clarity)
- Amendment that goes beyond simple combination (e.g. adds wording from description, modifies a feature) → clarity examinable, but only for the amendment-introduced issue
- **Fix clarity at examination stage** — there is no second chance

**Where it fits in the system**: G 3/14 closed a 30-year split in Board jurisprudence (compare T 459/09 vs T 459/03). It is now the leading authority on clarity in opposition.

**Cross-ref**: Guidelines D-V-3, H-II-3.1.

### G 1/03 and G 2/03 (8 April 2004) — Disclaimers
**Question**: Under what conditions may an undisclosed disclaimer be added to a claim?

**Holding** (summarised): undisclosed disclaimers are admissible to:
1. Restore novelty over Art. 54(3) prior art (earlier-filed, later-published EP/PCT applications)
2. Restore novelty over an accidental anticipation (one so unrelated and remote that the skilled person would never have considered it)
3. Disclaim subject-matter excluded from patentability under Art. 52(2), Art. 53

**Constraints**:
- The disclaimer must not provide a technical contribution
- It must not remove more than is necessary
- It must be clearly defined (Art. 84 clarity)

→ Refined by **G 1/16** (2017): "gold standard" of Art. 123(2) applies to disclosed disclaimers; "G 1/03 standard" applies to undisclosed disclaimers.

---

### G 2/10 (30 August 2011) — Disclosed disclaimers
**Holding**: a disclaimer that excludes specifically disclosed subject-matter from a claim must satisfy the standard test under Art. 123(2) — i.e. the remaining subject-matter after the disclaimer must be directly and unambiguously derivable from the application as filed (the "gold standard").

---

### G 1/16 (18 December 2017) — Two standards for disclaimers
**Holding**: confirms two distinct standards:
- For **undisclosed** disclaimers → G 1/03 conditions apply
- For **disclosed** disclaimers → G 2/10 gold-standard applies

This separated decisional approaches that had become muddled.

---

### G 2/21 (23 March 2023) — Plausibility / evidence for technical effect
**Holding** (briefly): evidence filed after the priority date may be relied upon to support a technical effect provided that the skilled person, on the basis of the application as filed and common general knowledge, would derive that effect as being encompassed by the disclosure.

**Relevance to clarity**: indirectly — it affects how Art. 56 inventive step is assessed for technical-effect-defined claims, which has knock-on effects on how broadly an effect-based claim can be supported (Art. 84 support arm) and enabled (Art. 83).

## Technical Board Decisions — by topic

### Relative terms — "substantially", "about", "approximately"

- **T 0337/95** — "approximately" rejected as unclear where no measurable tolerance is defined
- **T 0860/93** — "about" admissible where the field has an established convention
- **T 1582/08** — "substantially" admissible in a precise structural context

### Result to be achieved

- **T 0068/85** — leading early decision; functional language acceptable only if the function defines what is achieved AND the skilled person can readily perform it
- **T 0939/92** — claims defined by effect: must be supported across the breadth
- **T 1173/97** — software inventions; effect must be technical

### Parameters

- **T 0094/82** — unusual parameters: must be measurable; the method must be reproducible
- **T 0728/98** — parameter expressed as functional limitation: admissible if (a) it has clear meaning, (b) it can be reliably determined, (c) the skilled person can apply it without inventive effort
- **T 0276/06** — parameter requiring instrument-specific calibration: not clear unless calibration is standardised
- **T 1845/14** — composite parameters: each component must be measurable; combination must be reproducible

### Functional features

- **T 0068/85** — see above
- **T 0401/95** — pure means-plus-function (US-style) acceptable only where structural definition is genuinely unavailable
- **T 1067/97** — function clearly defined + skilled person can reproduce → admissible

### Essential features omitted

- **T 0032/82** — essential features missing from claim → Art. 84 objection
- **T 0133/85** — confirmed
- **T 0922/05** — applied to a chemical claim missing a process condition essential to reproducibility

### Inconsistencies between claims and description — description adaptation

- **T 1989/18** (16 December 2020) — Board: Art. 84 does **not** require the description to be amended to mark embodiments outside the claim scope. The clarity of the claims is judged on the claims themselves; description amendments are only required if the description renders the claims unclear.
- **T 1024/18** — Board took the **opposite** view: description must be adapted to remove inconsistencies; Art. 84 support requires it.
- **T 1473/19** — Aligned with T 1024/18: description adaptation is required.
- **T 1399/17** — followed the stricter position
- **T 2766/17** — followed the stricter position
- **T 56/21** (2023) — referred the question to the Enlarged Board → **G 1/24** (pending)

**Current practitioner posture**: comply with the examiner's adaptation request to obtain grant; preserve appeal options on key files. Once G 1/24 issues, harmonisation will occur.

→ Cross-ref: [F-IV § 4.3](epo-guidelines-f-iv.md#f-iv-43--inconsistencies-between-claims-and-description)

### Product-by-process

- **T 0150/82** — product-by-process is a claim to the product itself; novelty assessed on the product, not the process
- **T 0205/83** — confirmed
- **T 0411/89** — "obtainable by" = "obtained by" for novelty
- **T 0150/82** also clarified product-by-process is admissible only when the product cannot be defined otherwise

### Disclaimers (cross-ref Enlarged Board above)

- **T 0507/99** — early application of G 1/03
- **T 1107/06** — undisclosed disclaimer carving out remote prior art
- **T 1872/14** — disclosed disclaimer gold-standard

### Trademarks

- **T 0270/11** — trademark admissible where composition is constant and described in the specification
- **T 0480/98** — trademark unclear where product composition varies regionally

### Two-part form

- **T 0917/91** — two-part form not mandatory; one-part claims acceptable
- **T 0094/82** — two-part form recommended where prior art is well defined

### Reach-through claims

- **T 1063/06** — reach-through claims to compounds defined by activity at target: refused under Art. 83 + Art. 84
- **T 1329/04** — broad functional definition without enabling disclosure: insufficient

### Support / Art. 84 support arm vs Art. 83 sufficiency

- **T 0409/91** — broad claim, narrow disclosure: unsupported AND insufficient
- **T 0435/91** — same issue
- **T 0939/92** — generic claim with one example: must justify extrapolation
- **T 1727/12** — interplay of Art. 83 and Art. 84 in chemistry

### Second medical use

- **G 5/83** — Swiss-form for first medical use (historic; EPC 1973)
- **G 2/08** — EPC 2000 form ("X for use in treatment of Y") replaced Swiss-form for second medical use

### Computer-implemented inventions

- **T 1173/97** — technical character requirement; clarity intertwined with patentable subject-matter
- **G 3/08** — Enlarged Board found referral inadmissible but commented on the framework

### Apparatus "for" / method "for"

- **T 0287/86** — "apparatus for" vs "method for"; "for" in apparatus claims is generally non-limiting
- **T 0401/95** — confirmed

## How to Use Case Law in a Response

1. **Identify the objection precisely** — what Guidelines section is cited?
2. **Look up matching Board decisions** — see the categories above
3. **Cite Board decisions in the response** — they are persuasive to the Examining Division because deviation invites appeal
4. **Distinguish hostile case law** — if the examiner cites a decision unfavourable to you, distinguish on the facts
5. **Be cautious with Boards of different composition** — Boards are not bound by each other; cite preferably **multiple converging decisions** or the **Enlarged Board** where available
