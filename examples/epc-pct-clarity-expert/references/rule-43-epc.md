# Rule 43 EPC + Rule 6 PCT — Form and Content of Claims

These rules govern the **formal** requirements for claims. They complement Articles 84 EPC and 6 PCT (substantive requirements). Many "clarity" objections actually arise under Rule 43 / Rule 6 rather than the parent articles — practitioners should know both.

---

## Rule 43 EPC — Form and Content of Claims

### Rule 43(1) — Two-part form
> "The claims shall define the matter for which protection is sought in terms of the technical features of the invention. Wherever appropriate, claims shall contain:
> (a) a statement indicating the designation of the subject-matter of the invention and those technical features which are necessary for the definition of the claimed subject-matter but which, in combination, are part of the prior art ("preamble");
> (b) a characterising portion — beginning with the expression 'characterised in that' or 'characterised by' and specifying the technical features for which, in combination with the features stated under sub-paragraph (a), protection is sought."

**Practice notes**:
- Two-part form is **recommended where appropriate**, not mandatory (Guidelines F-IV-3.7)
- Useful where closest prior art is well-defined; misleading where it isn't
- Misplaced features (genuinely novel features in preamble) attract examiner attention

### Rule 43(2) — Multiple independent claims of the same category
> "Without prejudice to Article 82, a European patent application may contain more than one independent claim in the same category (product, process, apparatus or use) only if the subject-matter of the application involves one of the following:
> (a) a plurality of interrelated products,
> (b) different uses of a product or apparatus,
> (c) alternative solutions to a particular problem, where it is inappropriate to cover these alternatives by a single claim."

**Practice notes**:
- Critical at examination — examiners rigorously enforce this
- If your application has multiple Type A apparatus + Type B apparatus claims → justify under (a/b/c)
- (a) "interrelated products" example: transmitter + receiver; key + lock; plug + socket
- (b) "different uses" example: same compound used as antibacterial vs antiviral
- (c) "alternative solutions" example: two genuinely different ways of solving the same technical problem, each requiring a structurally different solution

### Rule 43(3) — Dependent claim form
> "Any claim stating the essential features of an invention may be followed by one or more claims concerning particular embodiments of that invention."

### Rule 43(4) — Dependent claim references
> "Any claim which includes all the features of any other claim (dependent claim) shall contain, if possible at the beginning, a reference to the other claim and then state the additional features. A dependent claim directly referring to another dependent claim shall also be admissible. All dependent claims referring back to a single previous claim, and all dependent claims referring back to several previous claims, shall be grouped together to the extent and in the most practical way possible."

**Practice notes**:
- References must be precise: "Apparatus according to claim 1, wherein …"
- Multi-dependent references: "Apparatus according to any of claims 1–5, wherein …"
- Group dependents logically; reading order matters

### Rule 43(5) — Reasonable number
> "The number of claims shall be reasonable with regard to the nature of the invention claimed."

**Practice notes**:
- No fixed upper limit
- Beyond 15 claims, claim fees apply per Rule 45 EPC
- Examiner may object to needless multiplicity (cross-ref Art. 84 conciseness)

### Rule 43(6) — Avoid references to description/drawings
> "Except where absolutely necessary, claims shall not, in respect of the technical features of the invention, rely on references to the description or drawings. In particular, they shall not rely on such references as: 'as described in part … of the description', or 'as illustrated in figure … of the drawings'."

**Practice notes**:
- Acceptable where the geometric content is genuinely undefinable in words
- Convert "as shown in Fig. 3" → recite the geometric content
- Even where allowed, examiners scrutinise carefully

### Rule 43(7) — Reference signs
> "If the European patent application contains drawings including reference signs, the technical features mentioned in the claims shall preferably, if the intelligibility of the claim can thereby be increased, be followed by such reference signs relating to these features, placed in parentheses. **These reference signs shall not be construed as limiting the claim**."

**Practice notes**:
- Required where drawings exist and intelligibility is improved
- Format: `a piston (12)`, `said piston (12)`
- Final sentence is **crucial** — reference signs are not limiting in EPO practice; conflict in some national courts where signs may be interpretive

## Rule 6 PCT — Form and Content of Claims (International)

### Rule 6.1 — Number and numbering of claims
> "(a) The number of the claims shall be reasonable, considering the nature of the invention claimed.
> (b) If there are several claims, they shall be numbered consecutively in Arabic numerals."

Mirrors Rule 43(5) EPC.

### Rule 6.2 — References to descriptions or drawings
> "(a) Claims shall not, except where absolutely necessary, rely, in respect of the technical features of the invention, on references to the description or drawings."

Mirrors Rule 43(6) EPC.

### Rule 6.3 — Manner of claiming (two-part form)
> "(a) The definition of the matter for which protection is sought shall be in terms of the technical features of the invention.
> (b) Whenever appropriate, claims shall contain:
> (i) a statement indicating those technical features of the invention which are necessary for the definition of the claimed subject-matter but which, in combination, are part of the prior art,
> (ii) a characterising portion — preceded by the words 'characterized in that,' 'characterized by,' 'wherein the improvement comprises,' or any other words to the same effect — stating concisely the technical features which, in combination with the features stated under (i), it is desired to protect."

Mirrors Rule 43(1) EPC with the addition of US-style "wherein the improvement comprises" — a deliberate accommodation for US practitioners filing PCT.

### Rule 6.4 — Dependent claims
> "(a) Any claim which includes all the features of one or more other claims (claim in dependent form, hereinafter referred to as 'dependent claim') shall do so by a reference, if possible at the beginning, to the other claim or claims and shall then state the additional features claimed. Any dependent claim which refers to more than one other claim ('multiple dependent claim') shall refer to such claims in the alternative only. Multiple dependent claims shall not serve as a basis for any other multiple dependent claim."

**Practice notes** — important divergence from EPO:
- PCT Rule 6.4(a) prohibits "multiple dependent on multiple dependent" claims
- This is a US-influenced restriction (US patent practice has the same rule)
- For EP regional phase: EPO accepts both single and multiple dependencies, including multiple-on-multiple (Rule 43(4) EPC is more permissive)
- For US national phase: stricter rules apply (multiple dependents incur fees per claim count, and multiple-on-multiple chains are disallowed)
- **Drafting strategy**: avoid multiple-on-multiple dependencies at PCT stage if heading to US national phase; restructure during EP regional phase if needed

### Rule 6.5 — Utility models, etc.
National variations.

## Rule 45 EPC — Claims fees

Beyond 15 claims, additional claims attract fees per Rule 45 EPC (currently approximately €265 per claim from claim 16 to 50, and approximately €660 per claim from claim 51 onward — verify current EPO fee schedule).

**Drafting strategy**: consolidate dependent claims; remove trivial dependents; consider whether dependents from different independent claims can be unified.

## Rule 137 EPC — Amendment

Rule 137 governs **when** and **how** claims may be amended:
- **Rule 137(2)** — amendment in response to a Rule 70a search opinion or first examination communication: as of right
- **Rule 137(3)** — further amendments only with the consent of the Examining Division
- **Rule 137(5)** — restrictions on combining unsearched subject-matter with searched (e.g. lack-of-unity invention)

This matters for clarity work: late introduction of clarity-fixing amendments may be refused as inadmissible.

## Rule 63 EPC — Incomplete search

> "If the European Patent Office considers that the European patent application fails to comply with the EPC to such an extent that it is impossible to carry out a meaningful search regarding the state of the art on the basis of all or some of the subject-matter claimed, it shall either issue a reasoned declaration to that effect or, as far as is practicable, draw up a partial search report."

**Practice implication**: claims so unclear that a search cannot be performed → partial search or non-search declaration. This is rare but damaging — it weakens the application going into examination.

Common triggers:
- Pure result-to-be-achieved claims
- Reach-through claims defined only by activity at a target
- Excessive Markush groups with no functional unifying feature
- Claims defined by undefined parameters
