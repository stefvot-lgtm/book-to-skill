# Response Strategies — Overcoming Art. 84 Objections

How to respond to an Examining Division communication (Rule 71(3) EPC / Art. 94 EPC) or an opposition-related clarity issue. Every strategy maps to the **EPO Guidelines F-IV** sections and the leading case law from [case-law-clarity.md](case-law-clarity.md).

> The skeleton: **classify → cite → choose argument or amendment → preserve fallback**. Use the **Guidelines section the examiner cited** as the entry point.

---

## Step 1 — Classify the Objection

Read the communication carefully. Identify:
1. **Which Article** is cited (Art. 84 — clarity / conciseness / support? Or mixed with Art. 83 / Art. 123(2)?)
2. **Which Guidelines section** is cited (F-IV-4.6.1? F-IV-4.10? F-IV-6?)
3. **Which claim feature** is challenged
4. **What the examiner says is wrong** (vagueness? result-only? parameter? functional?)
5. **What outcome the examiner wants** (deletion? definition? amendment from description?)

| If the objection is about… | Go to … |
|---|---|
| "Substantially / about / approximately" | § 2.1 below |
| "Result to be achieved" | § 2.2 |
| Unusual parameter | § 2.3 |
| Functional feature | § 2.4 |
| Essential feature missing | § 2.5 |
| Trademark | § 2.6 |
| Inconsistency claim ↔ description | § 2.7 |
| Description adaptation (out-of-claim embodiments) | § 2.8 |
| Support / breadth | § 2.9 |
| Conciseness / claim count | § 2.10 |
| Disclaimer | § 2.11 |
| Clarity in opposition | § 3 |

## Step 2 — Objection-Specific Response Patterns

### 2.1 — "Substantially / About / Approximately" (F-IV-4.6.1)

**Three response options**:

**Option A — Define the tolerance in the claim**
> "Amend claim 1 by replacing 'substantially perpendicular' with 'perpendicular within ±5°'. Basis: para. [N] of the application as filed where this tolerance is disclosed."

**Option B — Argue established meaning**
> "The term 'substantially planar' has an established meaning in the field of optical surfaces, namely surface deviation below λ/4 at the operating wavelength, as evidenced by [textbook reference / standard]. Per Guidelines F-IV-4.6.1, an established meaning in the field is sufficient."

**Option C — Delete and rely on broader claim**
> "Amend claim 1 by deleting 'substantially'. The undefined narrower scope ('perpendicular') is encompassed by the broader claim and is supported by Example [N]."

**Pitfall**: amending may introduce Art. 123(2). Always check basis.

**Case law support**: T 1582/08, T 0860/93 (established meaning accepted); T 0337/95 (undefined "approximately" rejected).

---

### 2.2 — "Result to Be Achieved" (F-IV-4.10)

**Three response options**:

**Option A — Add the producing features**
Identify the structural or process features that produce the result, add them to the claim. The claim becomes feature-based, not result-based.

**Option B — Argue genuine necessity**
> "The claimed effect can only be defined functionally because the structural means span [N] structurally different embodiments shown in Examples 1–N, all producing the same effect by different mechanisms. Per T 0068/85, functional definition is acceptable where structural definition is genuinely impractical and the function is reproducibly performable."

This works only when (a) multiple structural means exist, (b) the function is clearly measurable.

**Option C — Restructure the claim**
> Convert from "A composition that lowers blood pressure" → "A composition comprising [feature X] in an amount of [range Y]" — making the structural feature primary and the effect a property.

**Case law**: T 0068/85, T 0939/92, T 1173/97.

**Often paired with**: Art. 83 sufficiency objection. Address both together.

---

### 2.3 — Unusual Parameter (F-IV-4.11)

**Response template**:

1. **Justify** the parameter: why is it the right way to characterise the invention?
2. **Specify** the measurement method completely:
   - Instrument (make/model or generic class)
   - Sample preparation
   - Conditions (temperature, humidity, time)
   - Calibration
3. **Cite** the method location in the description (or amend to include if missing — careful of Art. 123(2))
4. **Show reproducibility** with at least one example

**Argument template**:
> "The parameter X is defined by the measurement protocol set out in paragraph [N] of the description: [recite key steps]. This method is reproducible as demonstrated by Example [M] giving value Y ± Z. The skilled person can apply the method without inventive effort, satisfying Guidelines F-IV-4.11 and T 0728/98."

**Case law**: T 0094/82, T 0728/98, T 0276/06, T 1845/14.

**If method missing in the description**: difficult. Adding it post-filing risks Art. 123(2). Consider narrowing to a standard parameter instead.

---

### 2.4 — Functional Feature (F-IV-4.5, F-IV-4.10)

**Response options**:

**Option A — Pair with structural anchor**
Amend the claim to add a structural anchor: "a heating element [function] configured to maintain a temperature of [range]". Now the claim has both structure and function.

**Option B — Argue field convention**
Functional features are more readily accepted in some fields (electronics — "means for transmitting") than others (mechanical — "device adapted to compress"). Cite field practice.

**Option C — Provide multiple structural embodiments**
> "Examples 1–5 show five different structural means, each performing the claimed function. Per T 1067/97, functional language is admissible where the skilled person can readily identify means performing the function without inventive effort. Examples 1–5 demonstrate that range of means."

**Case law**: T 0068/85, T 0401/95, T 1067/97, T 0728/98.

---

### 2.5 — Essential Feature Missing (F-IV-4.5)

**Response**:

**Step 1**: assess whether the feature is truly essential.
- Was it described as essential in the application?
- Does the invention work without it?
- Is its omission what makes the claim broader than the invention?

**Step 2**:
- If genuinely essential → amend the claim to include it (with basis)
- If not essential → argue: "Feature X is one preferred embodiment but is not essential to solving the technical problem of [N]. The invention as described works without X, as shown by Example [M]."

**Case law**: T 0032/82, T 0133/85, T 0922/05.

**Pitfall**: arguing a feature is non-essential can later harm inventive step (if X was important to the technical effect).

---

### 2.6 — Trademark (F-IV-4.8)

**Response options**:

**Option A — Replace with composition / chemical name**
> "Amend by replacing 'Teflon®' with 'polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)'. Basis: para. [N] discloses the chemical identity."

**Option B — Argue constancy of composition**
> "The trademarked product Y has a stable, well-defined composition as described in paragraph [N]: [recite composition]. Per Guidelines F-IV-4.8, trademarks are acceptable where composition is constant. T 0270/11 supports this approach."

This works only if you have composition data in the description.

---

### 2.7 — Inconsistency Claim ↔ Description (F-IV-4.3)

**Standard approach**: align terminology. Examples:

- Claims say "fastener", description says "screw and bolt" → amend description to use "fastener" or add "fastener (screw, bolt, rivet)"
- Claims say "biasing element", description says "spring" → either narrow claim to "spring" or broaden description to "biasing element such as a spring"

**Pitfall**: harmonising can introduce Art. 123(2) issues. Check basis for every renaming.

---

### 2.8 — Description Adaptation: Embodiments Outside Claim Scope (F-IV-4.3 — current controversy)

**The conflict**: examiner says "embodiments E2 and E5 fall outside the claimed scope after amendment of claim 1; mark them as 'not according to the invention' or delete." Per T 1989/18, this may not be lawful; per T 1024/18 and T 1473/19, it is. **G 1/24 pending**.

**Practical response options**:

**Option A — Comply (recommended for speed)**
Mark out-of-scope embodiments as "comparative examples" or "not falling within the present invention". Acceptable to most examiners; preserves grant timeline.

**Option B — Argue T 1989/18**
> "We respectfully maintain that no description adaptation is required. The claims are clear in themselves. Per T 1989/18 [headnote], Article 84 does not require the description to be adapted to mark out-of-claim embodiments. We note the question is pending before the Enlarged Board (G 1/24, referral from T 56/21) and reserve our position."

**Option C — Hybrid**
Adapt the embodiments that are easy to mark; argue for those where adaptation creates Art. 123(2) risk.

**Strategy**: option A for most files (grant matters more than principle); option B for strategically important files where you may need appeal flexibility.

→ Cross-ref: [case-law-clarity.md § Description Adaptation](case-law-clarity.md#inconsistencies-between-claims-and-description--description-adaptation)

### 2.9 — Support / Breadth (F-IV-6, Art. 84 support arm)

**Response patterns**:

**Pattern A — Narrow the claim**
Reduce claim scope to what the description supports. Often the cleanest path.

**Pattern B — Show extrapolation justified**
> "Example 1 demonstrates effect X at parameter value Y; Example 2 at Y'; together they span the range claimed. The skilled person, applying common general knowledge of [field], would extrapolate across the range without inventive effort."

This requires a strong technical narrative — naked extrapolation rarely succeeds.

**Pattern C — Convert to selection invention with technical effect**
If the broad claim cannot be supported but a sub-range can, narrow to the sub-range and identify the technical effect that justifies the selection.

**Case law**: T 0409/91, T 0435/91, T 0939/92, T 1727/12.

**Frequently paired with**: Art. 83 sufficiency. Always check whether to respond on Art. 84 support, Art. 83, or both.

---

### 2.10 — Conciseness / Claim Count (F-IV-5)

**Response options**:
- Combine redundant dependent claims
- Delete trivial dependents that don't add inventive content
- Merge multiple independent claims of the same category where Rule 43(2) allows
- Argue Rule 43(2)(a/b/c) exception where genuine

**Note**: claim count beyond 15 attracts fees but is not per se objectionable. The examiner is objecting to **needless multiplicity**, not absolute count.

---

### 2.11 — Disclaimer Issues (G 1/03, G 2/03, G 1/16, G 2/10)

**Classify the disclaimer**:
- **Disclosed disclaimer** → apply **G 2/10 / gold standard**: remaining subject-matter must be directly and unambiguously derivable from the application as filed
- **Undisclosed disclaimer** → apply **G 1/03 conditions**: only to restore novelty over Art. 54(3), accidental anticipation, or to remove non-patentable subject-matter

**Response strategy**:
1. Identify the type
2. Cite the applicable standard
3. Show conditions met
4. Verify clarity of the disclaimer boundary

## Step 3 — Clarity in Opposition (G 3/14)

If the objection arises in opposition:

1. **Are the granted claims unchanged?** → Art. 84 NOT examinable per G 3/14. State: "Claim 1 as granted; per G 3/14, Art. 84 is not a ground available in opposition."
2. **Have the claims been amended in opposition?** → Art. 84 examinable, but **only for the amendment-introduced issue**:
   - If the amendment is a pure combination of granted claims → still not examinable
   - If the amendment introduces wording from the description or alters a granted feature → Art. 84 examinable for that specific change
3. **Strategy**: in opposition, prefer **combinations of granted claims** over substantive amendments. This preserves G 3/14 immunity from clarity attack.

## Step 4 — Argument Architecture

A well-structured Art. 84 response has:

```
1. Acknowledgement of the objection
   "The Examining Division has objected that claim 1 is unclear because [N]."

2. Identification of the Guidelines section / case law cited
   "The communication relies on Guidelines F-IV-4.6.1 and T 0337/95."

3. Response — choose:
   a) Amendment + basis
      "Claim 1 is amended as shown in the marked-up annex. Basis: para. [N], page [M], lines [L-L]."
   b) Argument + support
      "The term X has an established meaning of [definition] in the field, supported by [reference]. T 1582/08 (point [N]) confirmed the same approach for analogous wording."
   c) Both
      "Without prejudice, claim 1 is amended to remove the objected term; in the alternative, the term is supported by [reference] and T 1582/08."

4. Cross-check against other provisions
   "The amendment is based on para. [N] (Art. 123(2) compliant); does not extend protection (Art. 123(3) — not applicable in examination); remains novel over D1 and inventive over D1 + D2."

5. Fallback offer (optional, file-by-file judgement)
   "If the Examining Division maintains the objection, applicant is willing to enter the further restriction of [N]."
```

## Step 5 — Common Mistakes in Responses

| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Ignoring the cited Guidelines section | Examiner thinks you didn't read it |
| Adding wording from description without checking Art. 123(2) | Creates a new objection in the next round |
| Bare argument without citation | Persuasive value is low |
| Conflating Art. 83 and Art. 84 | Examiner can simply restate the objection |
| Using "the invention" or "the present invention" in the claim amendments | Adds F-IV-4.4 objection |
| Failing to address each objected feature explicitly | Examiner reissues the objection |
| Late filing of auxiliary requests | Risk of non-admission under Rule 137(3) EPC |

---

## Step 6 — Use of Auxiliary Requests

In EPO practice (Rule 137(3) EPC; Article 13 RPBA), auxiliary requests are a key tool:
- **Main request**: claim 1 as preferred
- **Auxiliary request 1**: amendment narrowing further
- **Auxiliary request 2**: deeper narrowing
- **Auxiliary request 3**: fallback to a position you can definitely defend

Auxiliary requests should be **filed early** (with the response to the first communication if material) to avoid admissibility objections under Article 13(2) RPBA 2020.

---

## Step 7 — Interaction with Oral Proceedings

If oral proceedings are summoned:
- Prepare for each objection: written argument + verbal summary
- Have auxiliary requests on hand
- Be ready to fall back during proceedings — Boards expect adaptive prosecution
- Bring case law extracts (electronic OK at EPO; some Divisions appreciate paper)
